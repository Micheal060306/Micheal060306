Gold Zari vs Silver Zari in Silk & Banarasi Sarees

By Micheal Praveen M · Sep 15, 2025 · 4 min read

Sarees are more than just garments — they are a reflection of tradition, artistry, and timeless elegance. Among the many elements that make a saree valuable, the zari work is one of the most important. But when it comes to selling your old silk sarees, one question often comes up: What is the difference between gold zari and silver zari, and how does it affect the saree's value?

At Om Sai Ram Pattu Center, trusted old silk saree buyers in Chennai, we meet customers every day who bring in Kanchipuram pattu sarees, wedding sarees, and even Banarasi sarees, wondering about their zari. Understanding the difference between gold zari and silver zari is key to knowing the real worth of your saree.

What is Zari?

Zari is the glittering metallic thread woven into sarees, most commonly seen in the borders, pallus, and motifs. Traditionally, zari is made from precious metals like silver and gold, which is why sarees with real zari hold high value even decades after they are woven. Today, some zari is made with imitation materials, which lowers the resale value.

When it comes to Kanchipuram silk sarees and Banarasi sarees, the zari work is often what sets them apart as heirloom pieces.

Gold Zari in Sarees

Gold zari is made of real silver threads coated with a thin layer of gold. In older sarees, this was done using pure techniques that have been passed down for generations.

Why it's valuable:

  • Shine & Prestige – Gold zari gives sarees a royal look, making them ideal for weddings and festive occasions.
  • Durability – The gold coating protects the silver base, keeping the shine intact for years.
  • High Resale Value – Old silk saree buyers in Chennai often pay more for sarees with gold zari because of the precious metal content.

Gold zari is commonly found in bridal Kanchipuram sarees and Banarasi sarees, which are known for their heavy borders and intricate golden motifs.

Silver Zari in Sarees

Silver zari is made of pure silver threads, sometimes left natural and sometimes lightly coated for extra shine. In older sarees, the silver zari often shows a reddish or coppery sheen when viewed from the reverse side — a key sign of authenticity.

Why it's valuable:

  • Rich Look – Silver zari gives sarees a subtle yet grand appearance.
  • Purity of Silver – Even torn sarees with silver zari are valued by old zari buyers for their silver content.
  • Wider Use – Many older Banarasi sarees and festive Kanchipuram sarees were woven with silver zari.

Today, silver zari sarees are highly sought after by secondhand silk saree buyers in Chennai because silver prices continue to rise.

Key Differences Between Gold Zari and Silver Zari

  1. Base Metal – Gold zari is silver coated with gold, while silver zari is pure silver.
  2. Color & Shine – Gold zari has a yellow-golden shine, while silver zari gives a white or coppery glow.
  3. Price – Sarees with gold zari usually fetch a slightly higher resale value, but silver zari sarees also command excellent rates due to rising silver prices.

Occasion – Gold zari is often used in wedding sarees, while silver zari is widely seen in everyday festive sarees and Banarasi sarees.

Why Banarasi Sarees Are Special

Banarasi sarees are among the most prized in India because of their intricate zari weaving. Whether woven with gold zari or silver zari, an authentic Banarasi saree is always valuable.
